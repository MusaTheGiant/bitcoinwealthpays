Bitcoin, BTCB and BNB Smart Chain

What this page covers

Understand which network and which token the practical guides actually use.

The presentation talks about Bitcoin throughout, and prices every slot in BTC. The wallet guides tell a slightly more specific story, and a beginner needs both halves to avoid an expensive mistake.

Source fact

The SafePal guide instructs the user to filter tokens by BNB Smart Chain and switch on BTCB, BNB and USDT (BEP-20). The MetaMask guide instructs the user to add the BNB Smart Chain network. The VALR guide walks through buying BNB, not BTC.

What that means

Bitcoin (BTC) runs on its own network, the Bitcoin Blockchain. Bitcoin's network cannot run smart contracts of the kind described here.

BNB Smart Chain is a different Blockchain that can run smart contracts. This is where the Bitcoin Wealth contract is deployed.

BTCB is a token on BNB Smart Chain that is intended to track the price of Bitcoin one for one. It is often called wrapped Bitcoin. Holding BTCB is not the same as holding Bitcoin on the Bitcoin network. It is a token on a different chain that represents Bitcoin, and it depends on whoever issues it continuing to back it.

BNB is the coin used to pay transaction fees on BNB Smart Chain. You need a small amount of it in your wallet or your transactions will not go through, even if you hold plenty of BTCB.

The mistake that loses money. Sending a token to the right address on the wrong network usually means it cannot be recovered. If a guide says BEP-20 or BNB Smart Chain, that is not a suggestion. Both the SafePal and MetaMask guides make the same point about confirming the network before sending, and the SafePal guide adds the practical habit: send a small test amount first before moving anything bigger.

Who Issues BTCB, And What Backs It

The deck does not cover this, so here is the answer from outside the supplied material. It is worth knowing, because it is the asset your slots are priced in.

Verify this yourself rather than taking it from a course. Everything below is public and checkable, and the links are given so you can.

  • Binance issues BTCB. It launched in 2019, first on Binance Chain and later as a BEP-20 token on BNB Smart Chain.
  • It is intended to be backed 1 to 1 by real Bitcoin held in Binance custody. One BTCB is meant to equal one BTC.
  • The reserves are published. Binance lists the Bitcoin reserve addresses and a Proof of Assets page, so anyone can compare the Bitcoin held against the BTCB issued.
  • The BTCB contract on BNB Smart Chain is 0x7130d2A12B9BCbFAe4f2634d864A1Ee1Ce3Ead9c, viewable on bscscan.com.

What that means for you

This is a custodial arrangement. Bitcoin sits with Binance and you hold a token representing it. That is a normal, widely used design, and BTCB is one of the longest running examples on BNB Smart Chain.

It does mean the token carries counterparty risk that Bitcoin on its own network does not: its value depends on Binance continuing to hold the reserves and honour redemption. Published reserve addresses make that far easier to monitor than a bank balance, which is a genuine advantage, but it is still trust in a company rather than trust in code alone.

None of this is unusual or hidden. It simply means that when a slot is priced at 0.001 BTC, what actually moves is 0.001 BTCB on BNB Smart Chain, and that is a token issued by Binance rather than Bitcoin on the Bitcoin network. Two different things, both real, worth not confusing.

Key takeaways

  • The presentation prices everything in BTC, the wallet guides use BTCB on BNB Smart Chain.
  • BTCB is a token representing Bitcoin on another chain, not Bitcoin itself.
  • You need a small amount of BNB in the wallet to pay transaction fees.
  • Sending on the wrong network usually means permanent loss. Test with a small amount first.
  • BTCB is issued by Binance and backed 1 to 1 by Bitcoin in its custody, with reserve addresses published.
