Questions Worth Asking

What this page covers

Leave with a specific, answerable list of questions to put to the programme, and know which ones you can check yourself.

A good course tells you what it could not teach you. These are the points where the supplied material is silent, incomplete, or inconsistent with itself. None of them are invented, and none are answered here, because answering them would mean guessing.

Treat this as your checklist rather than a warning list. Every item below is a fair question that any well-run programme should be able to answer, and several you can settle yourself in a few minutes on a Blockchain explorer.

Contradictions inside the material

1. Company or no company. The written explanation says the matrix is not managed by a company, admin, or server, and the deck lists no owner control and no company dependency. The same deck says "Since 2024, Bitcoin Wealth has been building a strong foundation" and uses "we" throughout. Both cannot be fully true.

2. Slot 9 entry cost. Printed as 0.356 BTC in the profit table. Every other reference says 0.256 BTC, and the profit column only works with 0.256.

3. Slot 10 profit. Printed as 3.584 BTC in one table and 3.854 BTC in another. The 61.342 BTC grand total only adds up with 3.584.

Things the material never explains

  • Royal Pool requirements. Subsequent Cycles and the Royal Pool covers how the pool works. The one detail still unstated is what "meeting the requirements" by the 21st involves beyond holding slot 3.
  • BTC versus BTCB. The deck prices in BTC while the wallet guides use BTCB, and never explains the difference. Bitcoin, BTCB and BNB Smart Chain now covers who issues BTCB and what backs it.
  • Time frames. No indication of how long any slot, cycle, or rank typically takes.
  • Non-completion. Nothing is said about what happens to a member whose matrix never fills, or whether the 0.001 BTC is recoverable.
  • Contract permissions. The claim of no admin control is checkable in the contract code, but the material provides no audit and names no auditor.
  • Total membership. No figure is given for how many members exist, at which slots, or how many have completed a cycle. This is the single number that would most inform a decision, and it should be visible on chain.

Most Of This Is Checkable

The encouraging part is how much of the list does not depend on anyone answering you at all. The contract is public, so member counts by slot, completion rates, the Royal Pool wallet and its monthly distributions are all visible on chain to anyone willing to look. Where the Money Actually Goes shows you exactly how.

The rest are ordinary questions: what qualifying requires, who audited the code, what happens if a matrix never fills. A programme confident in its answers should be glad to be asked, and the people who introduced you will usually know or can find out.

How to use this list. Take it to whoever introduced you, or to the community running the programme. Clear answers are a good sign. Answers you can verify yourself are a better one. And how readily people engage with the questions tells you something useful either way.

If you find an answer to any of these, or you think something in this site is wrong, write to bitcoinaccumulating@gmail.com and it will be corrected.

You are not being asked to be suspicious. You are being asked to be thorough, which is what anyone should be before putting money into anything.

Key takeaways

  • The material contradicts itself on whether a company exists behind the programme.
  • Two numerical typos exist in the deck, and both are provable from the deck's own totals.
  • Time frames and non-completion outcomes are not explained, and the exact Royal Pool requirements are not stated.
  • Much of the list is answerable on chain without asking anyone, using the method in Where the Money Actually Goes.
  • Asking these is thoroughness, not suspicion.
