What You Now Know

What this page covers

Consolidate the whole course into a single reference you can act on.

The mechanics

  • Bitcoin Wealth runs on a smart contract deployed at 0x1ad09b043E0Fe59243C9a18ee1c855bd7792Cd29, and the matrix is the payout logic inside that contract.
  • Twelve slots, doubling from 0.001 BTC to 2.048 BTC, totalling 4.095 BTC.
  • Slots 2 to 12 are auto entry, so your own outlay is 0.001 BTC plus fees.
  • A cycle is fourteen positions. Nine payments go to you, two to your upline, three to your downline.
  • Later cycles pay seven to your wallet and one to the Royal Pool.
  • Recycling re-enters you at the same slot so it can pay again.
  • Ranks run Ignite, Beginner, Leader, Champion, Grand Champion. The qualifier is sponsoring two people.

The claims

  • Unlimited earning potential.
  • A self-sustained reward cycle.
  • Total income of 36.855 BTC across twelve slots, and 61.342 BTC with recycling.
  • Fully decentralised, no admin control, instant automatic withdrawals.

The arithmetic

  • Income is always nine times the slot price, because nine of fourteen positions pay to you.
  • Fourteen units enter a cycle and fourteen leave it. The system distributes rather than generates.
  • The material describes no product, service, trading, mining, or external revenue of any kind.
  • One member's 32.761 BTC profit equals what other members paid in and did not get back. Reaching 61.342 BTC requires roughly 14 full ladders from other members, or tens of thousands of slot 1 entries.
  • Each layer is fourteen times the one above it, so around 93 percent of members are always in the newest layer, not yet paid.
  • A single slot 12 completion, funded purely by auto entry, would need a base on the order of 56 trillion entries.

The open questions

  • What does meeting the Royal Pool requirements by the 21st involve, beyond holding slot 3?
  • Who issues the BTCB and what backs it?
  • How many members exist, at which slots, and how many have completed a cycle?
  • Has the contract been audited, and by whom?
  • What happens if a matrix never fills?

That is the whole picture. You have the mechanics as the material describes them, the claims as the material makes them, the arithmetic that follows from the material's own numbers, and the questions the material leaves open. Nothing was hidden from you and nothing was decided for you.

So, Should You Join?

That is the question you probably arrived with, and here is the honest answer: nobody can give it to you. Not this site, not the person who sent it to you, and not anyone in a group chat. It depends on your circumstances, what you can afford to lose, what you understood in these pages, and what still feels unclear.

What this site was built to do is make sure the decision is actually yours. If you join, let it be because the pages made sense to you and you know what you are buying into. If you do not join, let that be for the same reason. Either way, you should be choosing from understanding rather than from enthusiasm or from fear.

First, well done. You worked through 17 topics on a subject most people never bother to understand before putting money into it. Whatever you decide next, you are better equipped than you were, and that was worth your time.

This Is Your Starting Point, Not Your Finish Line

Treat this site as your basic research, not the whole of it. Two reasons.

First, this site is built on the material available when it was written. Programmes change. Decks get updated, errors get corrected, and details get explained more fully. Two numerical errors are flagged in these pages precisely because material is not always perfect on the first pass, and the Royal Pool explanation only became available after the earlier pages were already written.

Second, no single source should ever be the only one you consult about your own money.

Where to look next

  • The person who shared this site with you. They can answer follow-up questions and put you in touch with people who have been in longer.
  • Live Zoom presentations. Ask when the next one runs. Live sessions let you ask a question and hear it answered in real time, which a document cannot do.
  • In-person presentations. These happen too. Being in a room with people who are already participating tells you things no slide deck will.
  • The Blockchain itself. The most reliable source of all, and the only one with no opinion. Where the Money Actually Goes shows you how to read it.

Take these questions with you

Turning up with specific questions is what separates real research from being talked at. Start with these:

  • What exactly does meeting the Royal Pool requirements by the 21st involve, beyond holding slot 3?
  • How many members are currently active, at which slots, and how many have completed a full cycle?
  • Has the contract been audited, and by whom?
  • What typically happens to someone whose matrix does not fill?

Take your time. There is no deadline on this decision, whatever anyone tells you. Ask everything you want to ask, as many times as you need, until you can explain the system to someone else in your own words. When you can do that, you are ready to decide, and not before.

Then make the call. It is yours to make.
